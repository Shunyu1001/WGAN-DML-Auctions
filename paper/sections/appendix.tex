\section{Additional Derivations and Results}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:orthogonal-remainder}}

Take expectations in equation~\eqref{eq:orthogonal-reserve-score}. Since
$\mathbb E_0T_h=\eta_0$, the asserted identity follows. Differentiating that
identity at $\eta=\eta_0$ cancels the derivative of $m_h$ with the derivative
of the residual term. Taylor expansion of $m_h(\theta,\eta_0)$ around $\eta$
then gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_0\psi_h-m_h(\theta,\eta_0)
=-\int_0^1(1-t)(\eta_0-\eta)^{\mathsf T}
\nabla_\eta^2m_h\{\theta,\eta+t(\eta_0-\eta)\}
(\eta_0-\eta)\,dt.
\end{equation}
On the stated region its Hessian is bounded, and its $GG$ entry is zero
because $m_h$ is linear in $G$. This proves
equation~\eqref{eq:anisotropic-remainder}. If $A=A_h$, the Taylor expansion
in $G$ is exact and the remainder vanishes for every $G$. If only $G=G_h$,
the generally nonzero $AA$ remainder remains. Thus the result is not
two-sided double robustness.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:empirical-local-rate}}

For fixed $h$, the signals $T_h=(S_h,K_h)^{\mathsf T}$, their first
$\theta$ derivatives, and their Lipschitz constants in $\theta$ are uniformly
bounded by constants depending only on $h$. For example,
$\partial_\theta S_h=K_h$ and
$\partial_\theta K_h=-(\theta-B)K_h/h^2$. Applying Hoeffding's inequality
to an $n^{-1}$-net of the compact interval $\Theta$ and bounding between grid
points by the Lipschitz envelopes gives, for each training fold,
\begin{align}
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
\|\widehat\eta_{E,h}^{(-k)}(\theta)-\eta_{0,h}(\theta)\|
&=O_p(\rho_n),\\
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
\|\partial_\theta\widehat\eta_{E,h}^{(-k)}(\theta)
-\partial_\theta\eta_{0,h}(\theta)\|
&=O_p(\rho_n).
\end{align}
The fold proportions and fixed $K$ make the bounds uniform across folds. The
same bounds hold for holdout averages of $T_h$ and its first derivative.
The generated averages and their first derivatives satisfy the same fixed
envelopes, even if the generated distribution is misspecified or the number of
draws is fixed. Since $w_k=O(n^{-1})$, replacing the empirical learner with its
calibration changes both displays by only $O(n^{-1})$.

Uniform convergence and overlap imply
$\inf_{k,\theta}\widehat A_h^{(-k)}(\theta)\geq a_h/2$ with probability
approaching one, so clipping is inactive. On this event all functions of the
nuisance in the score and its total derivative are uniformly bounded and
continuous on a compact set. Substitution of the preceding uniform rates
into the score and equation~\eqref{eq:total-score-derivative} proves uniform
convergence of $\widehat\Psi_n$ and $\widehat\Psi_n'$.

For completeness, uniform stochastic control can also be expressed through
the class $\{\psi_h(\cdot;\theta,a,g):\theta\in\Theta,
a\in[a_h/2,1],\ g\in[0,(\sqrt{2\pi}h)^{-1}]\}$. This class has three real
indices, bounded Lipschitz envelopes, and polynomial covering numbers. Its
empirical process is therefore stochastically equicontinuous; see
\citet{vandervaartwellner1996}. Estimated nuisance curves take values inside
this class. This uniform argument, rather than a pointwise Chebyshev bound
alone, justifies evaluations at nearby random roots.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-h-dml}}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:empirical-local-rate},
$\widehat\Psi_n\to_p m_{0,h}$ uniformly on $\Theta$. Outside any fixed
neighborhood of $\theta_{0,h}$, compactness and the unique-zero condition
bound $|m_{0,h}|$ away from zero. Inside a sufficiently small neighborhood,
uniform derivative convergence gives
$\widehat\Psi_n'\leq-j_h$ with probability approaching one. Uniform
convergence also preserves the positive and negative signs at the neighborhood
endpoints and at the endpoints of $\Theta$. The intermediate value theorem
then gives exactly one interior downward root and no roots outside that
neighborhood. Its bracket can be found on a grid containing the endpoints
and refined. Clipping is inactive and the derivative rejection criterion is
passed. Thus the failure probability tends to zero, and the selected
approximate root is consistent. This derives root consistency rather than
assuming it.

Write $P_{n,k}$ for the holdout empirical measure. At the fixed population
root, decompose
\begin{equation}
\widehat\Psi_n(\theta_{0,h})
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_h(B_i;\theta_{0,h},\eta_{0,h})
+R_{1n}+R_{2n},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
R_{1n}=\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{|I_k|}{n}(P_{n,k}-P)
\{\psi_h(\widehat\eta_h^{(-k)})-\psi_h(\eta_{0,h})\}.
\end{equation}
The common arguments $(B;\theta_{0,h})$ are suppressed in this display.
Conditional on training observations and independent training randomization,
the bracketed function is fixed for a holdout fold. Its conditional second
moment is $O_p(\rho_n^2)$ by bounded nuisance derivatives. The fixed number
of nondegenerate folds therefore gives
$R_{1n}=O_p(\rho_n/\sqrt n)=o_p(n^{-1/2})$; no independence between
different fold fits is required. Proposition~\ref{prop:orthogonal-remainder}
gives $R_{2n}=O_p(\rho_n^2)=o_p(n^{-1/2})$.

Let $e_n=\widehat\Psi_n(\widehat\theta_h)$, with
$|e_n|\leq\varepsilon_n=o(n^{-1/2})$. A mean-value expansion of the smooth
sample score between the consistent root and $\theta_{0,h}$ gives
\begin{equation}
e_n=\widehat\Psi_n(\theta_{0,h})
+\widehat\Psi_n'(\theta_n^*)
(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_{0,h}),
\end{equation}
where uniform derivative convergence and continuity imply
$\widehat\Psi_n'(\theta_n^*)\to_pJ_h$. Substitution establishes
equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-h-linearization}. The oracle score is bounded for
fixed $h$, has mean zero and positive variance, so the i.i.d. central limit
theorem applies. Uniform nuisance convergence, root consistency, and bounded
score envelopes also give consistency of the pooled second moment and
$\widehat J_h$. Slutsky's theorem gives the variance and Wald statements.
A centered difference with step tending to zero is consistent as well:
it averages $\widehat\Psi_n'$ over a shrinking interval, whose difference
from the continuous $m_{0,h}'$ tends uniformly to zero.
This specializes the cross-fitted Z-estimation argument of
\citet{chernozhukov2018} without assuming root or Jacobian consistency.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:score-set-coverage}}
\label{app:score-set-proof}

The fixed-target decomposition in the preceding proof gives
\[
\sqrt n\,\widehat\Psi_n(\theta_{0,h})
=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\psi_h(B_i;\theta_{0,h},\eta_{0,h})+o_p(1).
\]
The summands have mean zero and variance $V_h>0$. The fixed-$h$ bounded
envelope therefore gives the ordinary central limit theorem. Uniform
nuisance convergence and the local CDF lower bound also imply
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\{\widehat\psi_{i,h}(\theta_{0,h})
-\psi_h(B_i;\theta_{0,h},\eta_{0,h})\}^2=o_p(1).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and the law of large numbers then give consistency of the
pooled second moment. Since
$\widehat\Psi_n(\theta_{0,h})=O_p(n^{-1/2})$, centering the sample scores
and using divisor $n-1$ does not change the limit:
$\widehat V_n(\theta_{0,h})\to_pV_h$. This argument does not treat the
overlapping training folds as independent observations. Their estimated
scores are compared with the common i.i.d. oracle scores, and the orthogonal
remainder is controlled as in the preceding proof.

Slutsky's theorem yields
$T_n(\theta_{0,h})\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$. The probability that this
statistic is undefined at the true parameter tends to zero by $V_h>0$ and
variance consistency; the point-estimation failure probability also tends to
zero by Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-h-dml}. Consequently, treating failed
point-estimation runs as noncoverage does not alter the limit. Membership of
the true parameter in the continuous acceptance set is exactly its pointwise
nonrejection event, which proves the first coverage statement. No correction
for the number of candidate values is involved in that identity.

For every realized set, its interval envelope contains it. Hence
\[
\Pr\{\theta_{0,h}\in\mathcal E_{n,1-\gamma}\}
\geq
\Pr\{\theta_{0,h}\in\mathcal C_{n,1-\gamma}\},
\]
which proves the lower bound. Equality need not hold because the envelope
includes gaps rejected by the score test. The proposition gives no claim of
exact finite-sample coverage and no rate for errors from numerical detection
of acceptance components. Those errors and the implementation's numerical
failure rules are checked and reported separately. In particular, the
pointwise argument alone does not bound every additional failure event from
attempting to evaluate a complete finite-grid acceptance set.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:plugin-equivalence}}

The full-sample kernel averages and their first derivatives have the same
uniform rates. The preceding sign and derivative argument therefore supplies
a unique consistent plug-in root with probability approaching one. At
$\theta_{0,h}$, ordinary Taylor expansion in the two sample moments gives
\begin{equation}
m_h(\theta_{0,h},\widetilde\eta_h)
=\nabla_\eta m_h(\theta_{0,h},\eta_{0,h})^{\mathsf T}
(\widetilde\eta_h-\eta_{0,h})+O_p(\rho_n^2)
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_h(B_i;\theta_{0,h},\eta_{0,h})
+o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
The sample moment derivative converges uniformly to $m_{0,h}'$. Expansion at
the plug-in root yields the same asymptotic linear representation as the
cross-fitted empirical or calibrated root. Subtracting these representations
proves the asserted first-order equivalence. The conclusion concerns the
same fixed bandwidth and target; it does not compare an unsmoothed estimator
with a smoothed one.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:richardson-exact}}

The population Richardson combination satisfies
\begin{align}
r_{\mathrm R}(h)
&=\frac{\lambda^2\theta_{0,h}-\theta_{0,\lambda h}}
{\lambda^2-1}\\
&=r_0-\lambda^2b_4h^4+o(h^4),
\end{align}
because the $b_2h^2$ terms cancel. Joint asymptotic linearity at the two
bandwidths implies
\begin{equation}
\widehat r_{\mathrm R}-r_{\mathrm R}(h_n)
=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\frac{\lambda^2\varphi_{i,h_n}-\varphi_{i,\lambda h_n}}
{\lambda^2-1}
+o_p(s_{\mathrm R,n}/\sqrt n).
\end{equation}
The assumed triangular-array central limit theorem and covariance consistency
studentize this sum. Condition~\eqref{eq:richardson-rate} makes the remaining
$O(h_n^4)$ population bias negligible relative to
$s_{\mathrm R,n}/\sqrt n$. If $s_{\mathrm R,n}\asymp h_n^{-1/2}$, this
condition is $\sqrt{nh_n}\,h_n^4\to0$, equivalently $nh_n^9\to0$; the
additional condition $nh_n\to\infty$ supplies increasing local information.

\subsection{Disconnected-set diagnostics and reporting}
\label{app:disconnected-set-diagnostic}

In the independent fixed-bandwidth validation, repetitions 29 and 50 at
$n=500$ produce disconnected score sets for both the calibrated and
empirical-local estimators. Each contains a low-end component touching the
prespecified lower search bound, 0.45. The original point-estimation rule
still returns a unique accepted root. These four method--replication records
are retained as successful computations with disconnection and lower-boundary
flags; they are not recoded as failures.

The diagnosis reuses the saved data and generated samples without retraining.
For both replications and all three estimators, grids with 281, 561, 1,121,

comparisons, the largest endpoint change is below $2.4\times10^{-12}$.
Direct centered variances and the implemented second-moment formula agree
within $3.5\times10^{-13}$; 60-digit decimal aggregation of the same
double-precision individual scores agrees within $2.3\times10^{-13}$.
The latter checks summation accuracy, not arbitrary-precision evaluation of
the kernel functions. The affected CDF estimates remain above the clipping
threshold. Thus these cases do not support a numerical-root or floating-point
explanation for the disconnected sets.

At $\theta=0.45$, the calibrated score means are approximately 1.798 and
1.364 in the two replications, whereas their score standard deviations are
24.557 and 16.604. The resulting studentized statistics, 1.637 and 1.836,
fall below 1.96 even though the score means are far from zero. A single
held-out observation accounts for 99.27 and 99.76 percent of the centered
score sum of squares. In each case the smallest observed maximum is excluded
from the training fold that supplies its nuisance estimate. A small
training-fold CDF, amplified by the negative powers of $A$ in the score,
gives that held-out observation very high influence. The same pattern occurs
with empirical-local moments, so it is not specific to the GAN.

This phenomenon concerns finite-sample information and the shape of the
studentized score, not a second population optimum. When one score $M$
dominates the sum and variance, $\overline\psi\approx M/n$ and
$s_\psi\approx |M|/\sqrt n$, so the studentized statistic can be close to
$\operatorname{sign}(M)$ rather than diverging at a distant candidate value.
Oracle-moment comparisons reject the low-end candidate in both replications;
they are attribution diagnostics and do not enter the reported estimator.

One simple sensitivity check fixes the score-variance denominator at the
estimated root while retaining the nonlinear mean:
\[
T_n^{\mathrm{fix}}(\theta)
=\frac{\sqrt n\,\widehat\Psi_n(\theta)}
{\sqrt{\widehat V_n(\widehat\theta_h)}}.
\]
The same pointwise central limit and variance-consistency argument can give
asymptotic coverage for its inversion, but does not ensure finite-sample
improvement or global connectedness. On the saved validation replications
this modification removes the disconnected components, yet lowers
$n=500$ coverage for both calibrated and empirical-local estimation from
93 to 90 percent. No new data are drawn for this sensitivity check, and it
is not treated as another independent validation or adopted as the reporting
rule.

equation~\eqref{eq:score-confidence-set}, with explicit flags. Its optional
envelope in equation~\eqref{eq:score-envelope} supplies a single interval
without deleting accepted values. A disconnected set's reported total length

gaps. Any source-set disconnection and boundary flag remain visible when an
envelope is shown. The lower bound is not increased after inspecting these
replications, and no component or influential observation is removed to
obtain a shorter interval.
