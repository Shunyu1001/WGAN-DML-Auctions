\section{Auction Model and Target Parameter}

Consider auctions indexed by $j=1,\ldots,n$. In the baseline model each auction
has the same known number $N\geq2$ of risk-neutral bidders; $N$ remains fixed
as the number of auctions grows. Bidder
valuations satisfy
\begin{equation}
V_{ij}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}F_0,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,N,
\end{equation}
independently across auctions. Values are private, utilities are quasilinear,
and the second-price allocation and payment rules are enforced. The seller's
value from retaining the object is normalized to zero. The distribution $F_0$
is continuously differentiable on the positive real line with density $f_0$
and finite first moment. The observed data
are $O_j=(B_j,N)$, where
\begin{equation}
B_j=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}V_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Under truthful bidding in the second-price benchmark, $B_j$ is the highest
submitted bid. It must be observed without censoring or truncation for every
sampled auction, including auctions that do not result in a sale. It is not
the transaction price, which is the second-highest bid when the reserve does
not bind. The fixed bidder population and truthful-bidding conditions are
maintained restrictions, not conclusions from a good distributional fit.
Neither selective entry nor reserve-dependent participation is modeled here.

Assume a regular valuation distribution with a unique interior
revenue-maximizing reserve $r_0>0$ and $f_0(r_0)>0$. Its first-order condition is
\begin{equation}
1-F_0(r_0)-r_0 f_0(r_0)=0.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $r_0$ maximizes the single-bidder posted-price revenue
$r\{1-F_0(r)\}$. For $N$ bidders, expected revenue in a second-price auction
with common reserve $r$ is
\begin{align}
\mathcal R_N(r;F_0)
={}&r\{1-F_0(r)^N\}\\
&+\int_r^\infty
\left[1-F_0(t)^N-N\{1-F_0(t)\}F_0(t)^{N-1}\right]\,dt.
\end{align}
The economic target is the scalar $r_0$. The main inferential result below
instead concerns a precisely defined fixed-bandwidth regularized target,
$\theta_{0,h}$. Point identification of $r_0$ does not by itself imply regular
root-$n$ inference for it. Connecting the two targets requires additional
smoothness and a separate shrinking-bandwidth argument.
