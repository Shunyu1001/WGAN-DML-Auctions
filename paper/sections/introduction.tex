\section{Introduction}

Optimal-auction theory characterizes the seller's revenue-maximizing reserve
price as a functional of the latent distribution of bidder valuations
\citep{myerson1981}. In empirical settings, however, researchers and sellers
often observe only a transaction price, a winning bid, or a small number of
order statistics rather than the complete vector of private values. This paper
asks whether a flexible generative model can recover the economically relevant
features of the latent distribution while still permitting valid inference on
the reserve-price parameter.

This question is difficult for a reason that is easy to hide with global fit
statistics. If only the highest of $N$ valuations is observed, then the
probability of seeing an auction maximum below the reserve is $F_0(r_0)^N$.
The reserve can therefore depend on a region with very few observations even
when the observable maximum-bid distribution is well approximated overall.
A small finite-sample fitting criterion is not a certificate of the local
accuracy required by the optimal-reserve score. For a fixed bandwidth, a true
population Wasserstein distance does bound the smoothed local moments through
their Lipschitz constants; those constants deteriorate as the bandwidth shrinks.

The proposed framework treats the valuation distribution as an
infinite-dimensional nuisance parameter and the reserve price as the
low-dimensional parameter of interest. A structural WGAN-GP reproduces the
auction observation process: it generates independent bidder values, applies
the relevant order-statistic map, and matches the resulting observable
distribution to the data. Cross-fitting separates nuisance estimation from
score evaluation, while a Neyman-orthogonal correction reduces the first-order
sensitivity of the reserve-price estimator to errors in the learned
distribution.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it connects adversarial structural
estimation to the order-statistics approach in empirical auctions. The
generator respects the independent-private-values structure before applying
the same maximum operator observed in the data. Second, it derives an
observation-level orthogonal score for a smoothed reserve and gives
conditions for cross-fitted asymptotic linearity and valid fixed-bandwidth
inference. It distinguishes the full confidence set obtained by inverting
the studentized score from a conservative interval envelope that contains it.
For the empirical-local and fixed-pseudo-count calibrated learners,
bounded kernel classes and a separated single-crossing root supply primitive
conditions. The resulting estimators are first-order equivalent to the
same-kernel full-sample plug-in; no first-order efficiency gain from the GAN is
claimed. A pure WGAN learner requires additional local-rate conditions.
A covariance-aware Richardson combination remains a conditional extension
from that regularized target to the exact reserve. Third, it uses nested Monte
Carlo ablations to distinguish failures of global WGAN learning, local nuisance
rates, smoothing bias, and empirical root selection rather than attributing all
errors to the generative model.

The archived Monte Carlo results sharpen the role of WGAN-GP in this problem. It reproduces the
observable maximum distribution reasonably well but misses local tail moments
that matter for the reserve. Replacing only those score nuisances with
training-fold empirical moments restores near-oracle coverage for the
fixed-bandwidth target, and the paper verifies the required rate for that
learner under a local overlap condition. For the exact reserve, a stable
$n^{-0.2}$ bandwidth
path combined with Richardson extrapolation reduces bias, and a
three-bandwidth residual-bias envelope attains 96.5 percent coverage in the
reported design. More aggressive shrinkage creates multiple roots; taking the
union over all root chains increases interval length without improving
coverage.

An archived paired follow-up implements a simple hybrid by giving the WGAN the weight
of 50 pseudo-observations in the training-fold local moments. It substantially
reduces error relative to the raw WGAN score without changing the network or
loss, but tracks empirical-only estimation closely and does not establish an
incremental WGAN advantage. A subsequent nested-validation ablation tests an
eleven-parameter monotone generator and adaptive mixing separately and jointly.
Its mixed results do not establish a uniform gain over empirical benchmarks.
A retrospective initialization-only control nearly reproduces the lognormal
results, so those gains cannot be attributed to adversarial training alone.
The primary new validation fixes the single-crossing safeguards and
score-inversion rule before drawing 100 fresh samples at each of $n=500$
and $n=2000$.
At the smaller sample size, calibrated-GAN coverage changes from 91 percent
under Wald inference to 93 percent under score inversion, with similarly
modest changes for the empirical comparators. The small number of discordant
pairs does not establish a reliable improvement. Two samples produce
disconnected sets for the calibrated and empirical-local methods. All
components are retained, and boundary and disconnection flags remain visible
when a conservative interval envelope is supplied. At $n=2000$, both interval
constructions are close to the nominal level. The new results support a
transparent reporting rule, not a claim of uniformly accurate finite-sample
coverage or an incremental GAN advantage.

The formal coverage result concerns the fixed-bandwidth regularized reserve.
Exact-reserve intervals remain a bias-aware prototype whose uniform validity
requires additional conditions on the shrinking-bandwidth root process.
The older simulation tables precede the stricter numerical gates and are
retained separately from the independent validation rather than pooled with it.
