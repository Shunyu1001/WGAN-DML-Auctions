\begin{abstract}
This paper studies inference for optimal reserve prices when only auction maxima
are observed. Under symmetric independent private values, maxima identify the
latent value distribution, which is estimated globally by a structural
Wasserstein generative adversarial network with gradient penalty (WGAN-GP).
I derive a cross-fitted Neyman-orthogonal score for a fixed-bandwidth regularized
reserve. Primitive conditions cover training-fold empirical kernel moments and
a fixed-pseudo-count calibration, with an explicit single-crossing target and
failure rule. These estimators are first-order equivalent to a same-kernel
full-sample plug-in. Inference can use either a Wald interval or the full set
obtained by inverting the studentized score; a conservative interval envelope
is reported separately and does not replace that set. Fresh validation with
100 repetitions at each of two sample sizes finds a modest small-sample
coverage gain from score inversion, but does not establish a reliable advantage
over Wald inference or an incremental gain from the GAN. Two small-sample
replications produce disconnected, boundary-touching sets because sparse
lower-tail information makes score variance highly sensitive to individual
observations. All components and diagnostic flags are retained. Earlier
experiments illustrate how good global WGAN fit can coexist with inaccurate
local moments; they remain distinct from the fresh validation. Inference for
the exact unsmoothed reserve requires additional bandwidth and bias conditions
and is treated as an extension.
\end{abstract}
